# Architecture

- Gameplay, 3D components, assets, saves and combat rules are documented in `src/game/AGENTS.md`; read it before changing them. Why: detailed contracts belong next to their owning subsystem.
- Keep the TanStack Start route architecture and generated client boundaries. Why: the published app runs on the Worker runtime.
- Controller onboarding browser tests use the isolated test save profile and a simulated standard Gamepad API in scripts/tests; never inject real saves or replace menu handlers. Why: end-to-end input regressions must exercise real polling without changing player progress.
- Touch input uses normalized keyboard bindings and releases on cancellation/menus. Why: remapping and interrupted gestures must not leave movement or fire held.
- Vehicle sound/RPM profiles consume existing fleet tuning; never invent a gearbox, fuel or second health system. Why: telemetry must describe real supported systems.
- Skate attachments preserve the model-up frame when parented to foot bones. Why: differing rig rolls must not turn accessories into the foot.
- Vehicle race courses are routed over drivable ground (src/game/race-routing.ts: A* over the real terrain, rivers, towers, homes and garages) and baked per world scale by `bun scripts/bake-race-routes.ts` into race-routes.generated.ts; race-routing.test.ts fails if a world change blocks a baked route. Why: authored course shapes ran through lakes and buildings; routing at load is too slow.
- Supply roads have a surface class and furniture from src/game/road-dressing.ts (asphalt trunk roads with lane lines, gravel mountain routes, dirt forest/swamp roads; reflector posts, guard rails, hub-approach lamps, destination signs) and every resolved river crossing renders a bridge on exactly the deck deckAt walks on (RoadDressing.tsx). Why: decks used to be invisible and every road was the same dirt strip.
- Blender is installed by `bash scripts/setup-blender.sh` (bpy 5.x as a module at /opt/blender-py, plus a `blender` command; no display or GPU needed). Build models with `blender -b -P scripts/build-fleet.py -- public/models/fleet <id>` and preview any GLB with `blender -b -P scripts/render-glb.py -- <model.glb> <out.png> [yaw]` (Cycles CPU, ~10 s). The convoy trucks are the war rig (Actors.tsx Convoys) and race rivals pick fleet cars by race type (RaceCourse.tsx rivalCar). Why: authored models must be checked by eye before they ship.
- The look is set in two places: color-grade.ts (camera grade per region — gritty muted wilds, cyberpunk Neon City; GritGrade.tsx post pass on HIGH/ULTRA, `cssGrade` canvas filter on LOW/MEDIUM) and the key/fill balance in Scene (sun ×1.2, dimmer hemisphere and IBL). Props should come from authored models (fleet/foliage GLBs) or the detailed generators in organic-geometry.ts (organicRock, flowerClump, saguaro, barkTrunk, canopyCluster, steelDrum, tyre, scrapPanel) — never raw low-segment primitives. Why: flat light and 6-segment primitives were what made the world read as Roblox.
- Never share one geometry between instanced meshes through `<primitive object={geo} attach="geometry" />`: R3F attaches the same object twice and crashes on unmount ("Cannot convert undefined or null to object" in removeChild), taking the whole scene down. Pass it as the `geometry={geo}` prop instead. Why: the horizon islands did this and blanked the world.
- Prop shapes live in pure geometry modules, each keeping the unit footprint of the primitive it replaced so instancing code is unchanged: organic-geometry.ts (rocks, trunks, canopies, junk, chipped stone, fluted columns), street-geometry.ts (lamp pole/cobra head/lens, W-beam, rail post, delineator, water tank, HVAC unit, antenna mast), camp-geometry.ts (ridge tent, equipment case, cargo sled, crystal shard), wild-geometry.ts (fallen log, mushroom, lily pad, lotus, boardwalk section, basalt column, obsidian shard, dead snag, broken ruin pier). Reach for these before a raw box/cone/cylinder. Why: one-primitive props were most of the "Roblox" look.
- Neon City's grade gets its cyberpunk from colour (saturation, violet/cyan split, violet night bounce in Scene), not from crushed darks: a contrast/exposure grade at night blacked out the streets and the operator. color-grade.test.ts guards this. Why: checked on the owner's MEDIUM (CSS) tier.
- The third-person operator holds the authored weapon GLBs (OperatorModel HELD_MODEL, same files as ViewWeapon); weapon-props.ts boxes are only the loading/failed stand-in. Why: the box gun was on screen all the time.
- Look direction (owner, asked directly Oct 24): GRITTY REALISTIC everywhere, NEON CYBERPUNK in Neon City. color-grade.ts holds colour back in the wilds/cities (saturation ~0.8-0.9, firm contrast, crushed darks, cool shadows/warm highlights, grain, vignette); Neon City alone is saturated. Don't re-saturate the wilds from reference photos without the owner saying so. Why: a parallel pass made it lush; the owner chose gritty.
- Unreal core-test goldens (`unreal/Plugins/WorldFractureCore/Tests/*_golden.txt`) are generated, never committed: record them with `node unreal/tools/gen-goldens.mjs` (gitignored; CMake stops with that hint if one is missing). Keep other generated bulk out of the repo too. Why: the goldens reached ~60 MB of text and the Lovable preview build failed on every push after they grew (Oct 25).
- After changing prices or rules in the web build, re-record the goldens and run the Unreal core tests (`cmake -S unreal/Plugins/WorldFractureCore/Tests -B <dir> && cmake --build <dir> && ctest --test-dir <dir>`). Known drift (Oct 25): EncounterCoreTest mismatches in the Astravore fight only (the Hollow Star raid boss and its summoned troops are not ported to the encounter core yet). ShopCoreTest passes again (WFShopCore has 941efb7's weapon/armour tiers).
- Phone detection is `isTouchDevice()` (client-input.ts), never a bare `(pointer: coarse)`: Galaxy phones with an S Pen report a fine pointer, so the owner's phone ran the desktop path (no 30 fps cap, full post stack, desktop HUD). Phone CSS in styles.css is scoped under `html.wf-touch` (set by `markTouchDocument`), not a pointer media query.
- No KHR_materials_transmission (or `transmission` on materials) anywhere: it re-renders the scene every frame and blanked Nexus City on the owner's phone. Imported glass is alpha-blended (`scripts/strip-heavy-materials.py`; traffic LODs also lose specular/clearcoat with `--lite`; `noTransmission` guards at load). Phones never mount the EffectComposer; they get the CSS grade.
- The Android app never runs the offline service worker: a worker registered by the Oct 25 web build kept serving its cached build inside the APK, so later APKs ran old game code. MainActivity.dropStaleWebCache unregisters workers and clears Cache Storage once per install (keyed on lastUpdateTime) and reloads; offline-register.ts skips registration in the native app. Settings → Display shows the build commit (`__WF_BUILD__`) so a phone report can be matched to a build.
- Phones get the simple HUD by default (settings.simplePhoneHud; `html.wf-simple-touch` in styles.css): move stick, look drag, fire and enter / exit vehicle only, plus hull, ammo, crosshair, compass and the current objective. Owner, Oct 26: "only put walk, shooting, enter vehicle for now while I test". Add new phone buttons inside TouchControls' `!simple` block unless the owner asks for them on the simple screen.
- Map images are baked (`bun scripts/bake-terrain-maps.ts` -> public/maps/terrain/, run by the APK workflow) and used only while their fingerprint (terrain-map.ts mapFingerprint) matches the live terrain; otherwise the map paints itself as before. Re-bake after a terrain change. Why: painting the full map took 20-60 s on the owner's phone, which showed the blocky preview. Destination close-up labels go through badge-labels.ts (no overlaps; captions drop first, then names) so city maps stay readable.
- Lovable-hosted assets (`src/**/*.asset.json`, URLs under `/__l5e/`) are not in public/, so the APK must bundle them: the Android workflow runs `scripts/bundle-hosted-assets.mjs mobile/www`, which downloads each into the app at the same path. Check its "hosted assets: N/69 bundled" line in the workflow log; without it the phone showed fallback ferns/shrubs/rocks/logs and missed audio and menu art.
- Homes wear real claddings, not one plaster: `Design.cladding` (brick / siding / plaster) is a 2 cm outer skin so rooms keep plaster; gable roofs are shingle, foundations stone, chimneys brick (`Box.surface`, textures from weathered-surface.ts `claddingTexture`, world-space triplanar). `mergeByMat` makes one mesh per cladding. Riverdale's square maps its stone/tower/roof boxes the same way.
- World Fracture is moving to Godot (owner, Nov 1: "make everything look like my reference using Godot engine"): `godot-world-fracture/` (Godot 4.6, GDScript) is the real game project, separate from the `engine-samples/` comparison demos. Features move over one at a time; until a system lives there, the web game (src/) is the source of truth and `bun scripts/export-godot-data.ts` exports its data to `godot-world-fracture/generated/` (gitignored; raw image bytes use `.pngb`/`.jpgb` so Godot never imports or compresses them). Check the look off-device with Godot under xvfb (`--rendering-driver opengl3 -- --shot out.png [map:<id>] [sat]`, see its README). `.github/workflows/godot-world-fracture.yml` publishes the `godot-latest` APK release. Why: the owner wants the Godot build to be the game, built to their reference art.
- Godot 3D regions (`godot-world-fracture/scripts/region_3d.gd`, owner Nov 1 "3D City" / "Street View"): built only from exported data (`maps/<id>.json` with buildings `[x,z,hx,hz,yaw,use,h,name,metres]`, `maps/<id>.height.pngb` 257² 16-bit heights between `heights.lo/hi`, the baked data image for land cover, `floating` islands for the Storm Islands). Give new CartoBuilding entries their real height `m` (metres) or they fall back to the 0..1 tallness curve meant for city towers. Per-region sky/light lives in `_style_for` after the target frames (wilds gritty day + cracked planet, Nexus/Neon night + wet streets). Lands with no ground in the playable world (Crystal Badlands, The Shattered Reefs) are `src/game/new-lands.ts` (their own CartoSource), never added to terrain.ts. Why: one data path keeps the web maps, Godot maps and Godot 3D in agreement.
- Before telling the owner to download a large Files deliverable, flush and prove it: `cp` into `/mnt/documents` can leave the bytes in write cache (`du -m` reports 0 blocks while `ls -l` shows the full size and `unzip -t` passes), so run `sync`, then confirm `du -m` reports the real size and `md5sum` matches the source copy. Why: a 342 MB zip read back clean from cache yet had no allocated blocks, and the download failed (Nov 1).
- Killbox arena maps (owner's Killbox maps & modes doc, Nov 1) live in `src/game/killbox-arenas.ts`: each is hand-built data (roofs, walkways, cover, ledges, spawns, zones, fracture event) hanging at ARENA_ALTITUDE over empty sea, installed into the shared deck/wall/solid registries by `killbox-arena-install.ts` and drawn from the same data by `KillboxArena.tsx`. The open world is hidden while you are up there (Scene `arenaShown`), arena airspace never counts as `submerged`, rivals and squadmates are held on the roofs with `confine` (machines do not collide with walls), and falling below `killPlane` is a death. A run records its map in `run.arena` at start; playlists rotate through every map that lists the mode (`arenaForRun`, by the period's match count). Maps follow the owner's PvP maps board (Nov 2: Kaiju Complex, Fracture Floor, Killbox Prime, then Skydock, Neon Yard, Glass Spire, Riverfront, Solara Outpost, Frostveil Ring, Thalassia Dome, Void Rift; night, wet, neon trim, lit towers, tactical cover, multi-level). Built: Kaiju Complex (`look` neon-rooftops, KaijuComplexArena.tsx; first called Rooftop Run), Fracture Floor (nexus-grid) and Killbox Prime (prime-roof; one deck, two lower balconies, the Containment Lock ring), the last two drawn by PlazaArena.tsx with a per-look `STYLES` entry, and Skydock (`skydock`, SkydockArena.tsx: decks over a cloud sea at dusk, its own sky dome drawn after the world sky, the Cloud Bank event), and Neon Yard (`neon-yard`, NeonYardArena.tsx: every container built tier by tier from the collision box, climbable stacks, the Crane Shuffle dropping boxes from the gantry crane's height), and Glass Spire (`glass-spire`, GlassSpireArena.tsx: a glass tower top, the raised Spire Crown, sky gardens down each flank, the Spire Shatter bursting the glass panels into shards while blast shutters rise), and Riverfront (`riverfront`, RiverfrontArena.tsx: promenades either side of a river, road bridges and a footbridge between quays, the Flood Alert raising flood barriers in a downpour), and Solara Outpost (`solara-outpost`, SolaraOutpostArena.tsx: the first day map, a desert relay outpost on a terraced mesa with its own day sky and sun, the Solar Flare whiting out the sky, folding the panel arrays and raising heat shields), and Frostveil Ring (`frostveil-ring`, FrostveilRingArena.tsx: a frozen ring round a bottomless shaft under its own aurora sky, the beacon platform on four bridges, two watchtowers, the Whiteout freezing ice walls across the bridges; ArenaWeather has a `snow` kind), and Thalassia Dome (`thalassia-dome`, ThalassiaDomeArena.tsx: an undersea research dome with its own ocean, labs round a moon pool with a bridge, two galleries, the Pressure Breach raising bulkheads in red alarm light; ArenaWeather takes a `color`); after changing an arena re-run `bun scripts/export-godot-killbox.ts` (Godot's Killbox reads that JSON); each for all playlists but Fracture Royale. Arena matches are played by bots (owner, Nov 2: "AI bots to play with and play against"): killbox-bots.ts runs the ally bots and drives the rival machines' movement and fire (their world-AI fire is held off with `cool`), bots fight each other, route over walkways (`routeVia`, `stairsFrom`), and KillboxBots.tsx draws allies in the white-blue colourway (rivals wear RIVAL_COLORS via `colorway`); killbox-bots.test.ts plays whole matches on every arena. The "every arena" tests in killbox-arenas.test.ts hold every map to point symmetry, clear spawns/zones in both event states, no overlapping props, smooth walkways and separate airspace. Dress them with the authored Neon towers (InstancedGlb) for skylines, the helpers in arena-parts.ts (towerShell, shippingContainer, jerseyBarrier, hazard stripes) and ArenaWeather for rain; Duel/Skirmish stay on Grid-Iron. Parapets are found from the roofs (every edge with nothing walkable beyond it), never authored. Moving cover is `shifters`: `shifterLift` from the fracture clock drives the render, and `syncShifters` swaps their walls/solids in each frame (solid while more than half raised). Add a map as data plus tests in killbox-arenas.test.ts, never as a one-off scene. Why: the matches all ran on one open-world spot.
